# Programming and community design direction (STA-58)

Documentation status: active

Implementation status: Exploratory — synthesis for review and later specification; the linked prototypes are not production code.

## Direction to review

Stagecom should feel like one Theater product with two tempos: a recognizable public playbill for published programming, and a quieter workspace for the people making that programming happen. The signed-in Home is the person's cross-Theater Callsheet. Entering a named Theater shifts to its programming and people; a Theater Operator reaches shared decisions through a visible Operations entrance in that same structure. An Event is the place where its schedule, team, operational decisions, public presentation, and accepted-team conversation come together under separate authority rules.

This direction combines the [STA-59 calendar vocabulary](https://linear.app/stagecom/document/sta-59-theater-programming-calendar-decision-4f566f7d5b5e), [STA-60 information hierarchy](https://linear.app/stagecom/document/sta-60-member-and-theater-information-hierarchy-decision-120062d04bba), [STA-61 Rehearsal commitments](https://linear.app/stagecom/document/sta-61-rehearsal-request-and-commitment-decision-d2823eeb7d8d), and [STA-62 conversation and profile boundaries](https://linear.app/stagecom/document/sta-62-event-conversation-and-member-profile-boundaries-39ccc7f1f04b). STA-64 selected the Home structure after prototype review; STA-63 selected calendar views and interaction direction after prototype review. The composed experience and visual language still need uncoached validation.

Home always means the personal Callsheet. The Theater landing and its navigation use the Theater's actual name. The one-Theater case should be effortless; a second membership adds clearly named Theater entrances and labels on mixed personal items. Phone navigation keeps the Theater name, Calendar, Events, People, and authorized Operations reachable without hiding those destinations in Settings. Actions identify the relationship that grants authority, such as Cast Member, Producer, or Reviewer.

## Calendar and commitment interaction

The shared Calendar shows each scheduled activity once, including Performances, Event-linked Rehearsals, standalone Practices, classes, workshops, independent shifts, and resource reservations. Event containers do not add duplicate entries. An offsite activity remains visible without consuming the Primary Venue. A reservation without a program is a Schedule Block. At minimum, each card distinguishes time, title or group, type, Tentative or Confirmed state, resource use, and booking contact. Status and resource use need words or shape as well as color. A Member's access to the title, group, and contact is the _proposed_ STA-59 disclosure change; the current product still shows opaque occupancy to uninvolved Members.

The [anonymized September fixture and walkthrough](../../src/features/programming-calendar-prototype/README.md) exercise dense weekends, classes, offsite Practice, a multi-day blackout, tentative hold, and staffing without copying personal data. Daybook and dense month should carry into specification. Resource week remains only a comparison in the throwaway prototype. Hover or keyboard focus reveals quick detail; click, tap, or Enter opens a URL-addressable destination. Event-linked entries open that Event workspace with the activity selected and related work in context. Standalone activities and reservations open their own detail. The destination states the next action this viewer may take.

A Rehearsal request proceeds from leader-proposed Candidate Slots to Cast Availability Responses, an optional Operator-granted expiring exclusive hold, explicit selected-time confirmation from every required Cast Member, Producer submission of a Proposal Revision, and authorized review. Available is not confirmed; Tentative is not a committed Call. An unheld candidate does not appear on the shared Calendar. A move keeps the current confirmed slot while the replacement is reviewed, then swaps atomically on approval; rejection, withdrawal, or expiry preserves the original. A new unavailability marks a confirmed Event At Risk for human action. Operational Approval and public Publication remain independent.

## Community boundary

The Event conversation serves the accepted Event team and authorized Theater oversight. It can reference an Occurrence or decision record, but posting does not submit availability, confirm a Call, approve a Proposal, or clear Work Queue. Mentions and explicit announcements can create targeted Notifications from recorded domain events; ordinary replies do not alert the whole team. Pending invitees cannot read conversation. Former participants retain only their participation-period history while still active in the Theater; posting ends with their relationship. Moderation must preserve an audit trail.

People shows active Theater Members, with biography, skills, image, and contact details controlled by opt-in choices. Private or draft Event participation stays hidden unless the person deliberately makes it Member-visible. Public profile consent and a per-Event public Cast credit are separate. Affiliation and profile visibility are Theater-specific. General direct messages and social feeds are outside this direction.

## Visual language to test after the layout decisions

The current [design baseline](../../docs/design/design-baseline.md) supplies Cubano, Public Sans, warm paper, and three Stagecom source hues. Treat it as a starting candidate. Its public playbill and calm workspace distinction still fits the reviewed layouts, while the final identity and tokens can change substantially after comparison on actual screens.

1. Give published Theater and Event pages room for distinct poster art, expressive display type, strong date and admission hierarchy, and a clear public-to-private workspace link when authorized. The poster is Event identity, not a status signal.
2. Keep Home, Event work, and Operations on a consistent reading grid with restrained surfaces, legible body type, and clearly labeled actions. Put imagery in discovery and profile contexts so operational decisions remain easy to scan.
3. Give Daybook and dense month the same semantic status language. Distinguish activity type, Tentative/Confirmed, resource use, conflict, and Publication through labels and layout before hue. Use the three existing accents as optional orientation cues for Theater, programming, and people; never make a hue itself the permission or schedule state.
4. Build a small comparison board on concrete Home, dense Calendar, Event, and public Event screenshots before replacing global tokens. Compare typography scale, card density, image treatment, focus indication, contrast, phone wrapping, and whether the same Event remains recognizable across public and private surfaces. Record the selected variant and revise `/dev/components` and `src/styles.css` together only in a later implementation slice.

No new palette or component library is approved by the low fidelity reviews. The child prototypes' fictional SVG posters and avatars demonstrate the need for Event and person identity; their artwork is not a required brand asset.

## Uncoached validation plan

Run sessions with Theater Operators, accepted Cast Members, and ordinary Members or close proxies. Begin from Home or a public Theater page, never a protected deep link. Use the one-Theater scenario first, then a multi-Theater and multi-role case. Repeat critical paths at desktop and phone width and allow participants to use their normal pointer, touch, or keyboard method. Record each path, wrong turns, words used to explain status and access, and any private detail they believe is visible.

| Participant task                                                                                              | Evidence to capture                                                                                                                                          |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Member finds a show, the Theater Calendar, its booking contact, and a collaborator profile.                   | Time and navigation path; whether they understand Daybook versus dense month and Member-visible versus private detail.                                       |
| Cast Member distinguishes a tentative Rehearsal request from a confirmed Call and reaches Event conversation. | Whether they know which response is required, whether availability alone commits them, and whether a message can change the schedule.                        |
| Operator finds a shared decision and previews a conflicted Event move without losing the current booking.     | Time to highest-priority work, recognition of Work Queue versus alert, interpretation of blackout and hold, and next action after opening the Calendar item. |
| Pending invitee and public visitor enter an Event; former participant revisits it.                            | Correct expectation about public snapshot, pre-acceptance redaction, conversation history, and route recovery.                                               |

The earlier operational-workspaces validation proposed a roughly 30-second highest-priority-work target, but [no uncoached sessions are recorded](operational-workspaces-validation.md). Use that as a test hypothesis, not a passing result. Keep anonymized participant notes and specific usability changes beside this direction; do not claim graduation from prototype walkthroughs or automated browser checks.

## Specification reconciliation

The existing [PRD](../../docs/product/PRD.md), [operational-workspaces spec](../../docs/specs/operational-workspaces.md), [permission model](../data/permissions-model.md), and [current implementation evidence](../features/operational-workspaces.md) describe a narrower shipped product. A later specification should resolve these changes together before production work:

| Proposed direction                                                             | Existing contract or capability to reconcile                                                                                                                                                                                                                             |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Programming-first Theater landing and Home discovery                           | Callsheet and Theater Operations currently prioritize operational work; retain the separate Work Queue, Operational Exception, and Notification meanings while changing presentation.                                                                                    |
| Member-visible title/group/contact, full activity mix, Daybook and dense month | Current Calendar redacts unrelated occupancy; performance Occurrences, holds, and Schedule Blocks do not cover standalone classes, workshops, Practices, or activity-level public visibility. Update domain model, permissions, queries, and public allowlists together. |
| Pre-review Rehearsal hold and approved-slot move                               | Current Counteroffer hold and approval invalidation do not provide this lifecycle. Define hold duration, withdrawal authority, response staleness, review survival on expiry, atomic swap, and public snapshot consequences.                                             |
| Event conversation and opt-in Member/public profiles                           | Current People directory shows names and badges; conversation, field-level consent, moderation, retention, and targeted message events need product and authorization contracts.                                                                                         |
| Visual tokens across public and private pages                                  | The implemented token baseline is a candidate; compare concrete layouts, then document an approved token and component contract before spreading it through production routes.                                                                                           |

The specification should split these into independently testable slices while keeping the public/private read boundary and application-level authorization intact. Do not infer new production capability from a prototype. No remote database change or release is part of STA-58.
